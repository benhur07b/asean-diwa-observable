## Key findings
<div>
Malaysia demonstrates a proactive approach to digital inclusion through national infrastructure projects like JENDELA and targeted entrepreneurship programs such as eUsahawan and the Strivers’ Hub. These initiatives aim to equip citizens, particularly women, with digital skills and access. However, this focus is not yet supported by a robust, overarching legal framework for gender equality. The absence of a standalone Gender Equality Policy and a low score on the World Bank's legal frameworks for women (47.5 out of 100) indicate that structural barriers remain. Policies like the MyDIGITAL Blueprint promote inclusion in broad terms but lack the specific, measurable targets needed for effective gender mainstreaming.
<br><br>
The country’s quantitative data is relatively comprehensive in some areas, offering insights into workforce participation, phone ownership, and even the gender pay gap for certain roles. Yet, it reveals concerning trends, such as a declining share of female graduates in STEM and ICT programs. A critical gap is the complete lack of data on enrolment and completion rates in ICT-related courses, making it difficult to analyze the educational pipeline. For ASEAN’s gender mainstreaming agenda, Malaysia's case shows that while entrepreneurship and infrastructure programs are vital, they must be complemented by foundational legal reforms and systematic, targeted data collection to address the root causes of the digital gender divide.
</div>

## Categorical indicators
<div>

| **<span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">INDICATOR</span>** | OBSERVATION |
| --- | --- |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Equality Policies**</span> | Malaysia does not have a comprehensive, standalone Gender Equality Policy. While Article 8 of the Federal Constitution guarantees equality, its practical application is limited without a specific legal framework. The country maintains reservations to key articles of the CEDAW convention. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Workplace Equality Policies**</span> | Based on World Bank's 2024 Women, Business, Law Report, the government has not yet published guidelines on nondiscrimination based on gender in recruitment in any job sector, including ICT. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender and ICT Policies**</span> | A dedicated policy for integrating gender into the ICT sector is lacking. The MyDIGITAL Blueprint promotes digital inclusion for women but does not include concrete gender-mainstreaming strategies or disaggregated targets. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Education and Development Initiatives**</span> | Malaysia runs several programs aimed at upskilling, such as the eUsahawan programme (2015) for micro-entrepreneurs and the Young Futuremakers Malaysia project (2022) to support youth employment in tech-oriented fields, with a focus on female TVET graduates. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Entrepreneurship Programs**</span> | Multiple initiatives support women entrepreneurs. The 1nita Project (2010) focused on rural women but appears to be inactive. The Strivers' Hub (2024) is a new digital platform launched by Mastercard and The Asia Foundation to provide resources and mentorship to 100,000 women-led MSMEs. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Budgeting**</span> | Malaysia has taken preliminary steps toward Gender Responsive Budgeting (GRB) since 2003, but it is not institutionalized across the government. A 2023 assessment noted that the lack of a legal mandate, disaggregated data, and technical capacity limits its effective implementation. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Specialized Government Bodies**</span> | While Malaysia has institutional mechanisms addressing gender equality and digital development, there is no specialized body or formalized institutional arrangement explicitly focused on the integration of gender and ICT. The Ministry of Women, Family and Community Development (KPWKM) leads gender equality initiatives, while the Ministry of Communications and Digital (KKD) oversees ICT policies and the digital economy. Gender Focal Points (GFPs) have been established across ministries to support the integration of gender considerations into sectoral policies, including ICT; however, these roles do not currently include a dedicated focus on gender and ICT integration. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Technology Access**</span> | The government has launched major national initiatives to improve connectivity. Jalinan Digital Negara (JENDELA) (2020) aims to achieve 100% broadband connectivity, and the Program Jaringan Prihatin (2021) provided subsidies to help low-income households access digital devices and internet connectivity during the COVID-19 pandemic. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Data Collection and Analysis**</span> | Malaysia has made strides in collecting gender-disaggregated ICT data through national surveys like the annual ICT Use and Access by Individuals and Households Survey by DOSM, but these efforts lack depth and consistency. The Statistics on Women Empowerment in Selected Domains, Malaysia 2024 highlights that while women have surpassed men in education and achieved near parity in health, significant gaps remain in economic participation and political empowerment. The Malaysia Gender Gap Index (MGGI) score of 0.705 in 2023 shows moderate progress, with urban centers performing better. However, without more comprehensive and ICT-specific data frameworks, it is difficult to develop effective gender-responsive digital policies. Improved data collection is essential to bridge digital gaps and support inclusive development.|

## Numerical indicators
<div>
Malaysia's quantitative data provides a mixed but informative picture. There is consistent, multi-year data for key indicators like internet access, phone ownership, workforce participation, and even leadership roles, allowing for trend analysis. However, significant gaps exist, most notably the complete absence of data on enrolment and completion rates for ICT-related courses, which obscures the view of the educational pipeline. Furthermore, some data points, such as those for management positions and the pay gap, come with specific limitations that narrow their scope.

| **<span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">INDICATOR</span>** | OBSERVATION |
| --- | --- |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Gap in Internet Access**</span> | Between 2013 and 2022, the gender gap in internet access fluctuated, ranging from a low of 0.83 to a high of 4.79. The gap started at 3.27 in 2013 and ended at 2.98 in 2022, showing no clear long-term trend of improvement. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Percentage of Women in the ICT Workforce**</span> | From 2021 to 2023, women consistently represented a majority of the ICT workforce. The share ranged from 66.5% to 69.7%, starting at 69.7% in 2021 and ending at 67.9% in 2023. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Percentage of Women in ICT Management Positions**</span> | Data shows that women's representation in general management roles is very low, though it is slowly increasing. The share rose from 2.9% in 2021 to 3.2% in 2023. It is important to note this data is not aggregated by sector. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Percentage of Women in ICT Leadership Roles**</span> | Women’s representation on the board of directors of companies registered with the Companies Commission of Malaysia showed a slight decline. The share was 79.4% in 2019 and decreased to 77% in 2021. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Pay Gap in ICT Sector**</span> | The gender pay gap for technicians and associate professionals was small but fluctuated. It was 2.0% in 2021, decreased to 1.9% in 2022, and then rose to 2.6% in 2023. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Percentage Women Graduates in STEM Fields**</span> | Between 2004 and 2018, the share of female STEM graduates showed a concerning downward trend. It started at 42.8% in 2004, peaked around 2012 at 45.3%, and then declined significantly to 34.17% in 2018. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Enrolment and Completion Rates in ICT-Related Courses**</span> | N/A |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Representation in ICT Startups**</span> | N/A |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Phone Ownership**</span> | From 2021 to 2023, phone ownership was nearly universal and the gap was minimal. Female ownership rose from 96.6% to 98.0%. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Proportion of Youth and Adults with ICT Skills**</span> | ICT skills showed steady improvement from 2015 to 2021. The ability to use a programming language, while still low, increased from 2.3% to 16.1%, and using mathematical formulas rose from 17.3% to 43.9%. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Share of Female Graduates by Field**</span> | Long-term data from 2004 to 2018 shows women are a strong majority in fields like Health and Welfare (72.4%), Education (69.7%), and Natural Sciences, Mathematics and Statistics (70.7%) as of 2018. However, they remain significantly underrepresented in Engineering, Manufacturing and Construction (27.1%). |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Share of Female Graduates in ICT Programs**</span> | The share of female graduates in ICT programs declined over time. It was 50.5% in 2004 and fell to 46.0% in 2018, indicating a decreasing interest or success rate for women in these programs.

## Gender Gap in Internet Access

<div class="card">
The difference between the percentage of women and the percentage of men who use the internet within a given population. 

A positive value indicates women generally have more access while a negative value indicates they generally have less.
</div>

```js
const indicators_gender_gap_internet_access = FileAttachment("../data/indicators_numerical_gender_gap_internet_access.csv").csv({typed: true});
```

```js
const indicators_gender_gap_internet_access_filtered = indicators_gender_gap_internet_access.filter(row => row.country === 'Malaysia')
  .map((row) => {
    return { 
      year: (new Date(parseInt(row.year), 0, 1)), 
      indicator_value: parseFloat(row.indicator_value)
    }
  })
```

```js
Plot.plot({
    x: {interval: Plot.timeInterval("1 year")},
    marks: [
        Plot.barY(
            indicators_gender_gap_internet_access_filtered, 
            {x: "year", y: "indicator_value", r: 16, fill: "#C8057C",  tip: true}),
        Plot.axisX({label: "Year"}),
        Plot.axisY({label: "Gender Gap in Internet Access (%)"}),
    ]
})
```
