## Key findings
<div>
While there are numerous qualitative initiatives aimed at empowering women and girls in ICT in Cambodia, there is a near-total absence of quantitative data to measure their progress or impact. Programs like Technovation, targeted scholarships, and entrepreneurship training demonstrate a clear intent to close the digital gender divide. However, the lack of systematic, gender-disaggregated data collection on ICT access, skills, and employment makes it impossible to assess the current landscape or track changes over time. This profound data deficit is the greatest barrier to understanding the true status of women in Cambodia's digital economy.
<br><br>
The implications for ASEAN’s gender and ICT mainstreaming agenda are significant. Cambodia's situation highlights that policy and program implementation alone are insufficient. Without the same commitment to data gathering, policymakers are essentially working in the dark. The country has foundational gender equality strategies in place, but their translation into the ICT sector remains undocumented. To align with ASEAN’s goals for an equitable digital future, Cambodia's first and most critical step must be to establish a framework for the regular collection of key gender-disaggregated ICT indicators.
</div>

These frameworks aim to advance gender equality broadly, but their specific impact on the ICT sector is not explicitly detailed. |

| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Education and Development Initiatives:**</span> | Cambodia is active in this area, hosting several targeted programs. These include Technovation (since 2014) to encourage young women in tech entrepreneurship, annual Girls in ICT Day events, and scholarships (2023) for disadvantaged women to pursue ICT degrees. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Entrepreneurship Programs:**</span> | Multiple programs are in place to support women entrepreneurs in the digital economy, including the Women ICT Frontier Initiative (2016) and a Capacity Building Programme on Digital Transformation for Women Entrepreneurs in Cambodia (2024). |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Budgeting:**</span> | There are no current gender budgeting and mainstreaming frameworks for Cambodia. In fact, the suggestion of utilizing gender-budgeting for their programs and policies are made in a few recent policy papers, namely the UNDP Cambodia Gender Equality Strategy 2025-2029 and the Universal Periodic Review of Cambodia - 4th Cycle by the Gender and Development for Cambodia. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Specialized Government Bodies:**</span> | Cambodia has two government bodies that act as the main promoter of gender equality and women's empowerment in Cambodia: the Ministry of Women's Affairs (MoWA) established in 1996 and the Cambodian National Council for Women (CNCW) established in 2001. However, there are no specialized government bodies, separate or under the two previously mentioned, that specialize for gender and ICT. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Technology Access:**</span> | Cambodia has implemented project-based initiatives to improve technology access. The Development Innovations project (2013-2019), funded by USAID, supported local tech solutions to development challenges and improved access to mobile and internet technologies. More recently, the Innovative ICT-Integrated Pilot Classroom project (2021) provided schools with smart classroom equipment and teacher training to enhance digital education. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Data Collection and Analysis:**</span> | There are no initiatives on gender-aggregated ICT data, and even during the numerical data collection there are few sources on regular and reliable gender-aggregated ICT data for Cambodia. The closest thing for data collection that exists is analysis on skills gap assessment for ICT.

## Numerical indicators
<div>
The quantitative data for Cambodia is exceptionally sparse, rendering a comprehensive analysis impossible. Nearly all key indicators (including women's participation in the ICT workforce, management roles, the gender pay gap, and graduation rates) are entirely missing. The few data points that are available are from a single year or are very dated, which prevents any form of trend analysis. This severe lack of data is a fundamental challenge to identifying gaps, crafting policy, and measuring progress.

## Gender Gap in Internet Access

<div class="card">
The difference between the percentage of women and the percentage of men who use the internet within a given population. 

A positive value indicates women generally have more access while a negative value indicates they generally have less.
</div>

```js
const indicators_gender_gap_internet_access = FileAttachment("../data/indicators_numerical_gender_gap_internet_access.csv").csv({typed: true});
```

```js
const indicators_gender_gap_internet_access_filtered = indicators_gender_gap_internet_access.filter(row => row.country === 'Cambodia')
  .map((row) => {
    return { 
      year: (new Date(parseInt(row.year), 0, 1)), 
      indicator_value: parseFloat(row.indicator_value)
    }
  })
```

```js
Plot.plot({
    x: {interval: Plot.timeInterval("1 year")},
    marks: [
        Plot.barY(
            indicators_gender_gap_internet_access_filtered, 
            {x: "year", y: "indicator_value", r: 16, fill: "#C8057C",  tip: true}),
        Plot.axisX({label: "Year"}),
        Plot.axisY({label: "Gender Gap in Internet Access (%)"}),
    ]
})
```
