## Key findings
<div>
Lao PDR has established a progressive policy foundation for gender equality, including a constitutional mandate for gender-responsive budgeting and a series of five-year national action plans. However, these high-level commitments have not yet translated into targeted actions within the ICT sector. While there are programs to support women entrepreneurs, specific initiatives to develop women's ICT skills are notably absent. This policy gap is reflected in the quantitative data, which shows a persistent and severe underrepresentation of women in STEM and technical fields of study.
<br><br>
The country’s data landscape reveals a critical disconnect. While long-term data on female graduates by field is available and paints a clear picture of educational disparity, there is a complete absence of data on women's participation in the ICT workforce, leadership roles, or the gender pay gap. This makes it impossible to assess whether educational trends are carrying over into employment. For ASEAN’s gender mainstreaming agenda, Laos’ experience demonstrates that while foundational gender equality policies are essential, they are insufficient without sector-specific strategies and the corresponding data to monitor outcomes in critical growth areas like the digital economy.
</div>

| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Equality Policies**</span> | Lao PDR has a strong policy framework, including The Fourth Five-Year National Plan of Action on Gender Equality (2021-2025) and the UNDP's Gender Equality Strategy (2022-2026). |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Workplace Equality Policies**</span> | While there are policies and strategies that aim to promote gender equality, there are no data found on gender equality policies specific to the ICT sector. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender and ICT Policies**</span> | While there are policies that relate Gender Equality and ICT, there are no specific policies for ICT development in Lao PDR that addresses gender disparity in the ICT. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Education and Development Initiatives**</span> | While there are initiatives that target skill development in ICT, there are no specific ones that are catered towards Women. In 2023, under the International Labour Organization (ILO)-Chinia Partnership Programme, a workshop was conducted for the development of skills for the ICT sector. In 2020, there was also the first ever ICT Competency Standards for Teachers (ICT-SCT) in Lao PDR. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Entrepreneurship Programs**</span> | Lao PDR has hosted training to support women entrepreneurs in using ICT, including the National Training on Women ICT Frontier Initiative in 2019 and another workshop in 2024 to empower women through digital transformation. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Gender Budgeting**</span> | Lao PDR is proactive in this area. An amendment to the Law on State Budget in 2015 legally mandates that government expenditures must ensure gender equality. This was supported by a National Workshop on Gender-Responsive Budgeting in 2016. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Specialized Government Bodies**</span> | Lao PDR has no specialized government body for Gender and ICT. The closest body that fulfills this function is the National Commission for the Advancement of Women (NCAW) established in 2003. It is tasked with promoting gender equality and the advancement of women. This covers all sectors, not just ICT. Additional note: Lao PDR has the MICT, which stands for Ministry of Information, Culture, and Tourism - not Information, Communication, and Technology. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Technology Access**</span> | Initiatives to improve technology access are primarily focused on the education sector in rural areas. Programs like the Basic Education Computer Learning Centre (since 2014) and Aide et Action Laos (2020) have provided computer equipment and digital literacy training for teachers and students. |
| <span style="color: rgb(200, 5, 124);">**Data Collection and Analysis**</span> | While Laos has gender-disaggregated data on basic ICT statistics like internet and phone access, they come from major surveys in the country like the Labour Force Survey and the Lao Social Indicator Survey, or even from international organizations like UNICEF's Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey programme. However, Lao PDR has recognized this issue on the lack of gender-aggregated data on ICT access, skills, and industry participation. The strengthening of their national gender statistics and improvements in its quality, accessibility, and comprehensiveness has been discussed in analysis as a priority solution. |

## Numerical indicators
<div>
The quantitative data for Lao PDR is highly uneven. While there is extensive, long-term data on the share of female graduates across various fields, there is a complete lack of data on the entire ICT workforce, from entry-level to leadership, as well as the gender pay gap. Data on basic access and ICT skills is very limited, making it difficult to assess the broader digital landscape. This profile (strong on educational outputs but silent on workforce outcomes) highlights a major gap in the country's data collection strategy.

## Women Graduates in STEM Fields

<div class="card">
The proportion of women among all graduates in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) fields, expressed as a percentage.
</div>

```js

```

```js
const indicators_women_graduates_in_stem_filtered = indicators_women_graduates_in_stem.filter(row => row.country === 'Lao PDR')
  .map((row) => {
    return { 
      year: (new Date(parseInt(row.year), 0, 1)), 
      indicator_value: parseFloat(row.indicator_value)
    }
  })
```

